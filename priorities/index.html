Your Real Priorities.

Know what you actually want, and your dream life stops being a dream and starts being a plan.

How it works: four short exercises, about 25 minutes. Answer with 1–3 words, honestly rather than nicely; there are no right answers. At the end you get your priorities as a PDF. Everything stays on this device, so you can pause and come back any time.

Exercise 2.2

Where are your missing pieces?

What you long for most usually points to what has felt missing. These gaps aren't flaws; they are clues to what matters to you. Rate each of the 8 areas of life as it feels today, then add 1–3 words on what's missing where the score is low.

Your biggest gaps

Your lowest scores come first. The three lowest are marked in gold.
For every area with a score of 5 or less, answer one question: does this gap hurt?
Hurts = it matters to you, you just aren't living it yet. Don't mind = it's simply not important to you right now.
Rate the areas above and your ranking appears here.

Exercise 2.3

What do you actually value and prioritize in your life?

Not what you think you should care about: what your life already shows. Answer each of the 9 questions with 1–3 keywords. Your pattern appears below as you type.

Your emerging pattern

We sorted your words into themes. The number shows in how many of your 9 answers a theme appears. Under each theme you see the words you wrote. Your three strongest themes are marked in gold.
Answer a few questions and your themes appear here.

Group what belongs together

Some of your themes belong together. Drag them into a group and give the group a name in your own words. That name becomes one of your priorities.
On a phone: tap a theme, then tap the group. Need a name? Press Suggest a name in the group. The outlined chips are your biggest gaps from 2.2. Gold = it hurts. Grey = you don't mind. Drag a gap into the group it belongs to; that shows you which priority will close the gap.
Now tap the group it belongs to.

Your group names are copied into exercise 2.4 automatically. You can still change the words there.

Now compare: does your strongest group contain a gap that hurts? Then you've found a priority you want but aren't living yet. A strong group with no gap is a priority you already live.

Exercise 2.4

Declare your 3 or 4 highest priorities

Your group names appear here. Rewrite them in your own words, short and clear. Three is enough; the fourth is optional. Use the arrows to put them in the order that feels right.

Well done

Knowing your priorities is the first step toward a life that gives you goosebumps.

When you live by your highest priorities, you stop pushing and start flowing. Decisions get lighter, energy returns, and the next chapter writes itself.

Redo this exercise every 4 to 6 months. Transformation brings clarity, and with it your priorities may shift too.

"Create my PDF report" builds a designed summary of all your findings: your priorities, your gaps, your pattern and your groups. Everything is saved automatically on this device only.
